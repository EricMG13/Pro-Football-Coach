PRO FOOTBALL COACH · DESIGN REFERENCE v2 · GROUP: CONTINUITY
Interrupted, and coming back
The six-lens review, on the persona "Casey": the library calls this "a commute game" twice in its own prose and designs for it zero times. Save cadence was implied at two values and ruled nowhere, nothing covered cold-launch resume, and a 10.5-minute match had no drawn exit — while the once-a-season draft clock got an expiry rule the 25-per-match call-in never did. Phase C of the uplift plan; lifts User Control and Freedom from 2.
Every rule here must respect 03 §1.3 — the render cannot change the outcome. Leaving a match and returning must land on the same play-by-play the seed already determined.
1  Four rules, stated once
C1  SAVE CADENCE
Every completed decision, and every week boundary. Not on a timer — a timer means the player can lose a decision they remember making. The save is small enough (2.1 MB) that per-decision is affordable, and P16 owns proving it stays so.
C2  COLD LAUNCH
Returns to the surface you left, not to the Week root. The Continue card names it. Restoring to a tab root is how a player loses their place and re-reads a screen they had already finished.
C3  LEAVING A MATCH
Always available, never destructive. The outcome is already determined by the seed (03 §1.3), so leaving forfeits nothing — it forfeits watching. Two ways back in and one way past.
C4  CALL-IN EXPIRY
Expiry is a deferral, not a dismissal. An unanswered call-in resolves to the coordinator's recommendation and says so afterwards. ~25 a match × a phone going in a pocket makes this the most-hit rule in the product.

The result is already decided — leaving does not change it, it only stops you watching. You lead 10–7 with 18 key moments left.
Simulating skips your remaining call-ins. They resolve to your coordinator's recommendation.
Three exits, because they are three different intentions
Keep watching is the default and the heaviest. Leave and resume is the commute answer — the train arrived. Simulate the rest is "I do not want to watch this one", which is a different feeling from "I have to go", and collapsing them into one control would serve neither.
"The result is already decided" is the load-bearing sentence
It is true — 03 §1.3 makes the render incapable of changing the outcome, and the seed fixed the play-by-play before the first snap. Saying it converts leaving from a risk into a preference. Without that sentence every player assumes leaving forfeits something, and the prior build's audit shows exactly what happens then: they force-quit instead.
Simulate names its cost, because it has one
It is the only exit that changes what the player gets — remaining call-ins resolve to the coordinator rather than to them. That is not a destructive action and does not need ConfirmSheet, but §7's rule that a refusal names the door applies to a shortcut too. One line under the buttons.

A call-in cannot resolve to nothing. The play happens either way, so an unanswered one resolves to the recommendation and reports what was chosen on the player's behalf. Silence would make the clock feel punitive; this makes it feel delegated.
BACKGROUNDED MID-CALL
PAUSED 0:08
The clock stopped when you left. It restarts when you come back.
The clock is a pacing device, not a reflex test. It pauses on background and resumes on foreground. A commute game whose eight-second timer runs while the phone is in a pocket is a game that punishes the commute it was designed for.
WHAT THIS GROUP SENDS BACK TO 04
§4 gains a persistence rule
Save on every completed decision and every week boundary, never on a timer. Cold launch restores the surface, not the tab root. The Continue card names both.
§5 gains a match exit
Three exits — keep watching, leave and resume, simulate. The sentence "the result is already decided" is required, not optional: it is what makes leaving safe rather than frightening.
§3 — CallInCard gains two states
Deferred (expired to the coordinator, reported afterwards) and paused (clock stops on background). Twenty-five a match makes these the most-hit states in the product.
A shortcut names its cost
§7 covers refusals. "Simulate the rest" is not a refusal and still changes what the player gets, so the rule should read: any control that changes the outcome set says so beneath itself.
