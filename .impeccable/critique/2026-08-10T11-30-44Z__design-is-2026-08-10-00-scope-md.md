## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** High product specificity, moderate documentation-layer sameness. The segmented `ScoreBug`, horizontal all-22 match view, broadcast/desk registers, generated team chrome, college-to-pro destination mutation, install-hour/contact/cap trade-offs, stakeholder voices, and commute-aware continuity belong to this game. The reference shell is much less specific: the same eyebrow, numbered sections, card grids, self-critique introductions, and `NOW IN 04` epilogues repeat across all sixteen sheets. The product UI feels authored; the documentation shell often feels formulaic and argumentative.

**Deterministic scan:** 39 warnings across the sixteen HTML files: 25 `side-tab`, 11 `em-dash-overuse`, and 3 `flat-type-hierarchy`. Most `side-tab` hits are false positives: broadcast corner marks, depth-chart spines, and failure-state callouts carry semantic information rather than decorative card accents. The three flat-hierarchy hits are also likely extraction false positives because the detector ignores larger sizes expressed through the `font` shorthand. The em-dash findings reinforce the independent observation that the sheets over-explain and repeatedly use the same rhetorical voice.

**Visual overlays:** No reliable user-visible overlay is available. Browser mutation was blocked by `BrowserUseSecurityError`; `detect.js` was not injected and no `impeccable` console messages were produced.

## Overall Impression

This is a strong visual world wrapped in an unreliable implementation library. The football-specific broadcast/desk split, consequence-first copy, continuity rules, and accessibility intent are significantly better than a conventional mobile-management mood board. The single biggest opportunity is editorial rather than stylistic: turn sixteen chronological arguments into one internally consistent, state-complete source of truth.

## What's Working

1. **Football-native visual grammar.** The score bug, field orientation, team-colour blocks, house/escalation system, and hard-edged broadcast furniture make the sport unmistakable without copying a competitor's layout.
2. **Consequence-first UX.** Decisions state their costs, refusals name the blocked door, reports lead with verdicts, and save failures preserve trust with specific recovery instructions.
3. **Accessibility and interruption are structural concerns.** AX5 reductions, composed VoiceOver sentences, Reduce Motion state sequences, save-on-decision, cold-launch restoration, paused call-ins, and leave/resume match behavior are designed into the system rather than appended as notes.

## Cognitive Load

Three of eight checks fail: one thing at a time, minimal choices, and progressive disclosure. The default game plan exposes 11 visible controls across tempo, pass depth, run gap, and run/pass balance. The roster header exposes five filter/sort choices. The five-item destination bar is defensible navigation but still exceeds the four-item working-memory guideline. Grouping, hierarchy, co-located evidence, and verdict-first copy keep the load moderate rather than high.

## Emotional Journey

Entry establishes ownership well, but the first-run sequence names an Offer screen and skips directly from job selection to appointment. The weekly loop builds competence through verdict-first planning. The match is the strongest peak: live furniture, call-ins, and lower thirds create tension without hiding state. Continuity and failure surfaces provide unusually good reassurance for commuters. The career climax is incomplete: promotion is framed as a meaningful trade, then ends at `Take the job` with no authored arrival, appointment, or visible world mutation. Career reflection remains largely guide-level, weakening the end memory.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The library is not one trustworthy authority

**Why it matters:** Implementers cannot know whether to trust a sheet, canon, or a later chronological patch. `Screens-v2` still says Inbox, Game Plan, Roster, AX5, light mode, `ErrorBanner`, and `EmptyState` are undrawn; later v2 sheets now draw them. `Components-v2` likewise says the failure set is unbuilt. `Throughput-v2` calls `ListControls` registry item 21 while canon defines a 22-component registry. More seriously, `Appearance-v2` claims all 22 field numerals become readable at 932x430 while canon says adjacent linemen remain 58% occluded and only 13 numerals ship; `Components-v2` applies `live` to a static final winner although canon forbids `live` on static content.

**Fix:** Reconcile every sheet against canon in one editorial pass. Remove historical “still missing” claims, resolve the field/numeral and static-`live` contradictions, correct registry numbering, and replace chronological epilogues with current implementation rules.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable document`

### [P1] The references violate their own accessibility contract

**Why it matters:** A reference library that demonstrates sub-contract controls and text will propagate defects into SwiftUI. Light-page tertiary labels use a role that the token sheet records below 4.5:1. Batch actions are 36 pt; recovery and empty-state actions are 36–38 pt. The AX5 destination bar remains at 12.5 pt despite the contract promising Dynamic Type throughout.

**Fix:** Restrict text roles to legal surfaces, give every action a measured 44x44 hit region, and either scale the fixed navigation chrome or explicitly document and test a narrowly justified exception.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

### [P1] Critical states remain missing or unresolved

**Why it matters:** SwiftUI implementation would have to invent product behavior at high-stakes moments. The Offer screen is named but not rendered; there is no regular-width 932x430 desk reference; the map's accessible form, timed draft expiry, rankings form, and post-promotion arrival remain unresolved.

**Fix:** Resolve each behavior in canon and draw the resulting state before implementation. Prioritize timed-draft interruption, map accessibility, Offer, and the post-promotion arrival; then add a regular-width desk reference.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P2] Recurring planning exposes too many choices at once

**Why it matters:** The fifteen-minute weekly loop asks users to scan 11 game-plan controls before every match. The design serves experts, but novices get a settings panel disguised as a coaching decision, and veterans lack reuse-last-plan or preset accelerators.

**Fix:** Lead with a coordinator recommendation or named preset, show the predicted trade-off, hide secondary axes under `Adjust`, and preserve the complete manual path plus reuse/compare shortcuts.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P2] The career's emotional climax stops too early

**Why it matters:** Promotion is the retention reset in the product strategy. Ending the moment on a button press under-delivers the reward for a multi-season investment and mirrors the missing Offer beat at the start of the career.

**Fix:** Render the offer confirmation and a short pro appointment/arrival that visibly changes bronze to steel, Recruit to Front Office, roster economics, and stakeholder context.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable delight`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex — impatient power user:** First-week teaching dims four destinations without a visible skip. Repeating 11 planning adjustments lacks presets, reuse-last-plan, or compare-to-last-week. The timed draft shows three proposals without exposing the full board/filter path. Roster search, multi-select, and consequence-labelled batch actions are strong.

**Sam — accessibility-dependent user:** Several actions miss the 44 pt target, light tertiary labels appear below the stated contrast floor, and the AX5 destination bar uses an undocumented fixed-size exception. `League-v2` calls map accessibility unresolved while `Accessibility-v2` later declares it hidden with a verdict twin; Sam needs one authoritative rule.

**Casey — distracted mobile user:** Save-on-decision, exact resume location, paused call-ins, and leave/resume match are excellent. Timed draft expiry is the dangerous gap: backgrounding the phone can affect a major decision without one settled behavior. Small actions are harder to hit while moving.

**Morgan — veteran football-management player:** Verdict-first recommendations risk solving the weekly analysis instead of helping Morgan reason. Ten attributes, three draft proposals, and three first jobs can feel curated unless deeper uncertainty and drill-down remain available. The absent post-promotion arrival undercuts the long-career payoff.

## Minor Observations

- SF Symbol names and several Dynamic Type assumptions remain explicitly unverified.
- Bronze and steel accents have near-identical luminance; the 9-degree geometry distinction may be too subtle at 44 pt.
- Holding progress UI for 600 ms can make near-threshold operations feel artificially slow.
- Light mode is competent but has sparse art-direction and reference coverage.
- The 44 pt destination bar has no tolerance beyond the minimum.
- The document layer overuses tiny uppercase eyebrows, numbered scaffolding, identical cards, meta-criticism, and em dashes.
