All 22, and why that was always the right reading of 04
04 §5.2 says the view foregrounds at most three marks and "everything else is drawn at reduced contrast and reduced size." v1 drew 14 and merged the line into one rounded rectangle. That was an over-reading: 01-RESEARCH.md §6.3's impossibility result is specifically about individually numbered marks at full field width, and its own arithmetic gives 7.5 pt between adjacent linemen — too tight for a legible numeral, not too tight for a disc. So: 22 discs, three of them foregrounded, numbers on all of them and legible on eleven.
The line merges optically, not by decree
The five linemen are 18 pt discs on 16 pt centres, so they overlap by 2 pt and read as one connected shape at a glance while still resolving into five bodies when looked at. That is what 04 §5.2's "the seven-man line is one shape" was reaching for, and it costs nothing to draw it honestly. The FM Mobile reference does the same thing — its discs overlap freely in congested areas.
Three foregrounded, and the matchup is drawn
Passer 09, receiver 84, the corner who decides it 24 — 26 pt, full opacity, ringed against the turf. The other nineteen sit at 50–62% opacity and 18 pt. The short lime rule between 84 and 24 is the matchup itself, which 04 §5.3 requires the view to be able to name.
Direction now reads, which was the open finding
v1's own return said nothing in a still frame told you which way the offence attacked. This is the answer to "would anyone know the sport?"
Cover every word on both frames. What remains: a green field with yard lines, hash marks and two coloured end zones; 22 numbered discs in two kits; a white line with a lime line ahead of it; a segmented bug with two team-coloured blocks and a black score; a slanted lower third with a jersey number and a big +24. It is unmistakably American football, and none of it is carried by copy. v1 failed exactly this test.
LowerThird, rebuilt as furniture
v1's was a rounded card with body text. This one takes the broadcast register: radius 0, a 9° skew, the player's number in the team's secondary against the primary, the yardage as numeral at display size in live, and a lime cap slash closing the right edge. Text is counter-skewed so it stays upright — the geometry is the graphic, not the type.
New finding: nothing gates two opponents against each other
02 §11.3.5 gates each programme's pair internally — ΔE against real trade dress, contrast within the pair. No rule stops two teams that meet from being mutually illegible. Drawing 22 discs makes that load-bearing: quantified, the drawn matchup is ΔE 59 apart and reads cleanly, but Ashgrove gold against Wexmoor's cream secondary is ΔE 31 — marginal at 18 pt. ΔE is the right tool, not WCAG contrast. Those two measure 1.65:1, which sounds catastrophic and is the wrong question: contrast asks whether text is readable on a background, ΔE asks whether two marks are different colours. 02 §11.3.5 already uses ΔE for trade dress, so the machinery exists. The engine needs a kit-clash rule at fixture time — a minimum ΔE between the two sides' on-field marks, with the away side falling back to its secondary. This did not surface in v1 because v1 never put both teams on screen in colour.

WHAT v2 SENDS BACK TO 04
§5.2 — all 22 are drawn
Amend "the seven-man line is one shape" to say the line is drawn as five or seven overlapping discs that read as one shape. The impossibility in §6.3 is about legible numerals, not about presence, and conflating the two cost v1 the whole frame.
§3 — ScoreBug is an eighteenth component
It is on the match view, the aftermath, the league readouts and the career line. v1 assembled it ad hoc from StatCells, which is how it ended up grey. Registry entry, or it will drift.
§2.1 — a kit-clash rule
Two opponents can each pass the pair contract and still be mutually illegible on the field. Needs a fixture-time ΔE between the two sides' on-field marks with a stated fallback. Belongs to generation; surfaced only by drawing both teams.
