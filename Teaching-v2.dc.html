Week one is the tutorial, and it is not a tutorial
D9 teaches "through the first real week rather than through cards". The FirstRun group drew the five screens before week one and stopped — so the part D9 actually specifies, the taught week itself, had no reference. That is not a missing screen. It is a state that the ordinary week screens are in, which is why P12 has to carry it from the start and P15 cannot bolt it on.
Guide only. D9's full beat sheet is in OPEN-DECISIONS.md and its pacing is an owner protocol. What is drawn is the mechanism: how a screen teaches without a coach mark.
1  Four devices, no coach marks
01  ONE THING LIVE
Everything not yet taught is present and dimmed, not hidden. The player sees the shape of the whole week from minute one and learns where things live by watching them wake up.
02  THE VOICE EXPLAINS
Instruction arrives as a StakeholderCard from someone with a reason to say it. A coordinator explaining the game plan is in fiction; a tooltip is not.
03  NO MODAL, EVER
Nothing blocks. The prior build's first-run tutorial clipped its own body text at XXXL on a 667 pt screen (AUDIT.md:291) — a card that cannot be dismissed and cannot be read.
04  IT ENDS SILENTLY
No "tutorial complete". The last dimmed thing wakes up and the week is ordinary. The player should not be able to name the moment onboarding finished.

The dimmed bar is the whole idea
Four of five destinations are visible and dimmed. The player learns the app has five places and that four of them do not matter yet — in one glance, with no card telling them so. Each wakes when its first real event arrives. Dimming is the tutorial.
A coordinator is a better teacher than a tooltip
"Twelve install hours — spend them on the plan and you have less for practice" teaches the week's core trade-off, states a real constraint, and is something a coordinator would say. The same sentence in a grey callout would be instruction. Here it is characterisation that happens to instruct, which is what D9 means by teaching through the week.
First-run state is a property of every week screen
The dim flag lives on the destination bar, the inbox items, and each tab's contents. That is state threaded through P12's whole surface set, not a P15 overlay. Building P12 without it and retrofitting later means touching every screen twice — the sequencing defect the review found in `05`, drawn.
