Card A — The five worlds (registry 27, WorldBackdrop)

The single variable that changes between screens. One is live at a time; it recedes into desk density without ever vanishing, so every screen still happens somewhere.

pitch — lit turf, tilted away from the viewer
pitchAfter — the same field, floodlights down
facility — the floor at night, two warm pools
desk — the world recedes; density rises
filmRoom — the one place the light goes cold

The rule this registers

  • A screen happens somewhere. No screen renders on a bare surface with no world beneath it — the world may recede to desk density, but desk is still a world, drawn with gradient because light falls off.
  • The world never carries data. Nothing about which world is live, nor any detail within it, encodes a fact the player must read. It is atmosphere, exactly as the mow band in §6.1 is ground texture rather than an information channel.
  • Light direction is constant. Every world's highlight falls from the upper left — the same direction a GlassPanel's sheen takes, so a panel and the world it sits over never contradict each other about where the light is.
  • filmRoom is the one exception to constant light: its throw is described as cold, from behind the viewer onto the screen, because it depicts a projector rather than daylight or floodlights. It is drawn that way deliberately, not by inconsistency.

VoiceOver — one sentence per row, written out

  • Pitch world: lit turf on a plane tilted away from the viewer, for any screen about the field.
  • Pitch-after world: the same field with the floodlights lowered, for the moment immediately following a match.
  • Facility world: the floor of the building at night under two warm light pools, for any screen about the programme.
  • Desk world: the world recedes into density, for management surfaces with the most information per screen.
  • Film room world: the one place the light goes cold, a projector's throw from behind the viewer, for opponent report and film review.

Card B — Three depth levels (registry 25, GlassPanel; the deep-panel contrast rule)

World, standard panel, deep panel — and no fourth level. A panel over a panel over a panel is a container pretending to be hierarchy.

Standard panel
White at 0.055. Labels and figures only — content.primary measures 2.69 here (the brightest turf), below the body floor.
Deep panel
#08070E at α ≥ 0.78. content.quiet measures 5.03 here at worst — clears the body floor. This panel may carry prose.

Body text over a lit world requires the deep panel — a contrast rule, not a taste one

  • The standard fill is white at 0.055. Composited over the brightest point of the lit pitch (#37A868) it resolves to #42AD70, on which content.primary measures 2.69 and content.secondary 1.42 — well below even the 3:1 non-text floor. A standard panel is for labels and figures, never prose, when it sits over a lit world.
  • The deep fill is #08070E at α ≥ 0.78 — not the 0.70 the prototype shipped, which left content.quiet at 4.30 over that same worst case. At 0.78 the panel composites to #122A22 over the brightest turf and to #08080F over night; content.primary / secondary / quiet measure 14.52 / 7.68 / 5.03 at worst — all clear the body floor.
  • At most two material panels are live per screen — the dominant object's and one secondary region's. Every other panel takes the opaque equivalent (world.work / world.raised from Card A of tokens-v4). A screen that wants a third has a composition problem, not a performance problem.
  • The blur cost is real. .ultraThinMaterial over a 3D-transformed world is the most expensive thing the product draws, and the P13 gate is a 16.7 ms frame.

VoiceOver — one sentence per row, written out

  • World level: the backdrop itself, atmosphere, carries no text.
  • Standard panel: white fill at 0.055 opacity: over the brightest lit world, content primary measures 2.69 to 1, below the body floor — labels and figures only.
  • Deep panel: fill 08070E at 0.78 opacity or greater: content quiet measures 5.03 to 1 at worst, clearing the body floor — may carry working prose.
  • Budget: at most two material panels live per screen; every other panel takes its opaque equivalent.

Card D — Grain (registry 26, GrainOverlay)

A fixed-seed noise tile drawn above every screen in .overlay blend at low opacity. Flat vector on flat dark is what reads as machine-made; grain is texture, not information.

Approximated here with repeated radial dots at 50% opacity in mix-blend-mode: overlay — the product's grain is a 128 px noise tile from a fixed seed, identical between runs so it can be cached; this CSS approximation is not that asset.

Grain and Reduce Transparency

  • Grain is dropped entirely under Reduce Transparency (04 §7) — it is a texture layer, not information, and removing it costs nothing a player needs.
  • Grain never encodes a value. Two renders of the same screen differ only in whatever the world or data changed; the grain tile is identical, which is why it can be cached rather than generated per frame.

Card E — Reduce Transparency, the flattened form (04 §7, added 2026-08-14)

Every panel takes its opaque equivalent, grain drops, the world becomes world.page. Composition and depth order survive unchanged — which is the whole reason §6.1 measures ratios against these opaque values rather than the composited glass in the first place.

Default — glass over the pitch world

Prep report
Deep glass at 0.78, sheen upper left, grain above.

Reduce Transparency — flattened

Prep report
Opaque world.work fill, no sheen, no grain. world.page replaces the pitch. Same corner shape, same ink, same ratios.

What must survive the flattening, stated as a test

  • Same composition. Every region present, at the same size and position.
  • Same depth order. What was in front stays in front, now expressed by fill contrast (raised over work over page) rather than by blur.
  • Same ratios. Every figure in Card A/B of tokens-v4 is measured against the opaque values, so this substitution changes nothing about legibility — it is not a degraded mode, it is the mode the numbers were always describing.
  • A surface with no Reduce Transparency branch has not had it considered — the same by-construction standard §7.1 already applies to the AX5 branch.
