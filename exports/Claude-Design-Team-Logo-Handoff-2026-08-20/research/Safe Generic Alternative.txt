Generic football rules, mechanics, and penalties cannot be copyrighted or trademarked. However, commercial branding, league names, specific trophy/award titles, and official event names are strictly protected trademarks.
Safe to Use (Generic Rules & Terminology)
Standard on-field rules, game mechanics, and generic off-season systems belong to the public domain of the sport:
* Penalties & Violations: Offside, False Start, Holding, Pass Interference, Facemask, Roughing the Passer, Intentional Grounding, Delay of Game, Ineligible Receiver Downfield, Neutral Zone Infraction, Illegal Shift/Motion.
* College-Specific Rules: Targeting, the 1-foot in-bounds catch rule, clock stoppage on first downs (or inside 2 minutes), 2-point conversions from 3rd OT onward.
* Gameplay Mechanics: Touchdown, Field Goal, Safety, Touchback, Fair Catch, Onside Kick, Two-Minute Warning (or Two-Minute Timeout), Spike, Kneeldown, RPO (Run-Pass Option), Play-Action, Zone Coverage, Man Coverage.
* Off-Field Concepts: Draft, Free Agency, Salary Cap, Franchise Tag, Waiver Wire, Transfer Portal, NIL (Name, Image, and Likeness), Redshirting, Walk-on, Signing Day.
Protected Trademarks to Avoid
1. Professional Football (NFL & Related Properties)
The NFL aggressively defends its terminology and visual identity:
Protected Trademark
	Safe Generic Alternative
	Super Bowl, The Big Game (commercial context)
	World Championship, Pro Championship, The Title Game
	Pro Bowl / Pro Bowl Games
	All-Star Game, Pro Showcase
	Lombardi Trophy
	Championship Trophy, The Gold Cup
	NFL, AFC, NFC
	Pro Football League, American Conference, National Conference
	NFL Draft, NFL Combine
	Pro Scouting Combine, Annual Rookie Draft
	Team Names & Nicknames (Chiefs, Packers, America's Team)
	Kansas City, Green Bay, or custom fictional mascots
	Official Logos & Color Schemes
	Custom palettes and generic vector logos
	2. College Football (NCAA, Conferences, & Bowl Committees)
College football IP is fragmented across several licensing bodies (NCAA, individual universities, conference offices, and independent bowl committees):
Protected Trademark
	Safe Generic Alternative
	NCAA, FBS, FCS, CFP (College Football Playoff)
	National Collegiate Association, Major College Division, College Championship Series
	Specific Bowls (Rose Bowl, Sugar Bowl, Fiesta Bowl, Orange Bowl)
	Pasadena Bowl, New Orleans Classic, Desert Bowl, Sunshine Classic
	Heisman Trophy, Biletnikoff, Doak Walker
	Player of the Year, Best Receiver Award, Top Running Back
	Conference Names (SEC, Big Ten, ACC, Big 12, Pac-12)
	Southeastern Division, Northern Ten, Atlantic Coastal League
	Specific Universities & Mascots (Crimson Tide, Buckeyes, Longhorns)
	Fictional school names, city/state designations (e.g., Tuscaloosa, Columbus, Austin), or editable school databases
	Player Likeness & Names (Right of Publicity)
* Pro Players: Represented by the NFLPA (NFL Players Association). You cannot use real player names, jersey numbers matched with team locations, or identifiable facial likenesses without a group licensing agreement.
* College Players: College athletes own their individual NIL rights. Using real college rosters requires individual NIL agreements or group licensing (e.g., via The Brandr Group or OneTeam Partners).
* Simulator Standard: Most indie football simulators use procedurally generated rosters (random names, randomized stats) and provide an in-game roster editor / CSV import feature so users can customize teams and players locally on their own devices.
