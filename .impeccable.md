## Design Context

### Users

Primary users are experienced mobile management-sim players who have exhausted football games they can trust over a long career. Secondary users are desktop football-sim players who want a credible, portable coaching game for a commute and accept less breadth in exchange for a world that remains coherent for decades. They play in focused roughly fifteen-minute weekly sessions: read what arrived, make consequential preparation and roster decisions, watch the match, and answer a bounded number of coaching moments. They are coaches, never on-field players; the interface must support judgement rather than arcade control.

### Brand Personality

Credible, deliberate, alive. The product should reward quiet competence, make stakes legible, and preserve belief in the simulated world over twenty seasons. Its writing is short, plain, and specific. Stakeholders speak in distinct human voices; system refusals name the exact limit, what clears it, and the control that resolves it. Every identity is fictional and original, with no real names, marks, colours, trade dress, photography, emoji, or placeholder Latin copy.

### Aesthetic Direction

Landscape-only iPhone UI with two disciplined visual registers. The DESK register closely proxies the owner-supplied Football Manager references: compact near-navy workspace, violet navigation and action furniture, 8–10 pt rounded opaque panels, 4–12 pt internal rhythm, restrained dividers, small but readable metadata, and dense two-pane hierarchy. The BROADCAST register is hard-edged and immediate: square geometry, generated team-colour fills, condensed tabular numerals, and television-sports composition for the live match, draft, signing day, and appointment. The reference may guide hierarchy, spacing, density and interaction behaviour, but the game retains fictional identities and original assets rather than copying marks, icons, photography or branded artwork. Light and dark appearances are equal requirements. Avoid portrait or desktop proportions, arcade-game styling, generic card dashboards, glassmorphism, neon AI aesthetics, decorative gradients and dark-theme shadows.

### Design Principles

1. Every screen is explicitly a place to decide or a place to look; readouts state their verdict before presenting evidence.
2. The game initiates: something meaningful arrives every week, and at least one item demands an answer with a visible cost.
3. Design for the 844 x 390 pt landscape floor first; height is scarce and two panes stay visible.
   Dense metadata, ratings and table cells may use 10–11 pt type and 24–28 pt rows; working prose
   remains 12 pt, while primary and irreversible controls remain at least 44 pt. AX5 expands and
   reflows the composition.
4. Use SF Symbols for repeated utilities and match controls; retain visible words for destinations,
   costs, consequences and irreversible decisions. Every icon-only control keeps an explicit
   accessibility label.
5. Separate live sport from management work rigorously: BROADCAST is square and team-coloured; DESK is rounded and neutral; chartreuse means now and nothing else.
6. Accessibility, legal originality, and measured surfaces are structural constraints: both appearances, AX5, Reduce Motion, VoiceOver sentences, composited contrast, and fictional identity are designed in rather than reviewed in later.
