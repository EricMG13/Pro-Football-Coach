# Impeccable — Pro Football Coach

## Design Context

### Users

Football management-sim players on iPhone. Mostly the orphaned *Football Coach* / *Pro Football Coach* community (r/FootballCoach) whose Android games died in 2019 and whose successor shipped Steam-only — they have been asking for an iOS pro sim for seven years. They know the genre deeply: they will read a dead-money number and argue with it.

Context of use: phone in hand, one-handed, in short bursts — a lunch break, a commute, ten minutes before bed. Sessions are either *fast* (advance the week, check standings, close the app) or *deep* (an hour inside free agency and the draft). The interface has to serve both without a mode switch.

The job to be done: **run a franchise and build something that lasts.** Not "watch a simulation" — make the call, see the consequence, live with it across a decade of seasons.

Emotional targets, in order:
- **Competence** — the numbers are real and the app respects that the player understands them.
- **Anticipation** — the weekly loop should make you want to hit Play.
- **Trust** — an autosave that never eats a season-8 dynasty is a design feature, not an engineering detail. Stability is the #1 complaint driver in the reference app; the UI should never look like it might lose your work.

### Brand personality

**Confident · plainspoken · durable.**

Voice: a good coordinator explaining a decision. Short declaratives, real numbers, no hype. "Cutting Reyes leaves $6.2M dead through 2028." Never "Uh oh! That's a big cap hit! 😬". Never marketing language, never exclamation marks on system copy, sentence case everywhere except deliberate broadcast-register displays.

The app is a professional instrument that happens to be fun, not a toy that happens to have spreadsheets.

### Aesthetic direction

**Native iOS restraint as the chassis; broadcast sports treatment on gameday moments only.** Two registers, one rule for which is which.

**Chassis (default — everything not listed below).** System grouped backgrounds, white cards at 20pt radius, SF Pro, generous whitespace, hairline dividers, pill chips for metadata, one concept per card. Color appears only where it carries meaning. This is what `Sources/ProFootballCoachUI/Theme/DesignSystem.swift` already encodes; extend it, don't fork it.

**Hero register (broadcast).** Deep team-color band, tabular heavy numerals, tight stat blocks, high-contrast W/L states. Permitted on exactly these surfaces:
- Season Hub "This Week" card
- Matchup Preview hero
- Live Game scoreboard + field view (and the On-the-Field arcade HUD)
- Game Report hero / final score
- Draft Day on-the-clock card
- Trophy Room, championship and Hall of Fame moments

Anywhere else — roster, cap sheet, contracts, settings, stats tables — stays on the chassis. If a new screen wants the hero register, it needs to earn a place on that list first.

**Team color: accents only.** Tint, primary CTAs, chips, hero gradients and the scoreboard band. Body surfaces stay neutral system backgrounds in every case. With 32 arbitrary fictional color pairs, immersive theming guarantees contrast failures; accents keep it legible in light and dark with no per-team correction table.

**Theme:** light and dark, both first-class. Settings exposes Light / Dark / System and neither mode is an afterthought.

**Anti-references — what this must never look like:**
- **The college reference app.** Legal boundary and product differentiation. Inspiration for information architecture only: no copied layouts, color choices, icon sets, or strings. Same for any real league's names, marks, or player names — everything is fictional and original.
- **Madden / EA glossy chrome.** No faux-3D bevels, metallic gradients, lens flares, aggressive italic skew.
- **A spreadsheet.** No dense multi-column table as a default view. Long scroll of focused cards beats a grid the player has to decode.
- **Generic AI dashboard.** No purple-gradient-on-white SaaS look, no default component-library card grid, no emoji standing in for icons — SF Symbols only.

### Design principles

1. **Calm chassis, loud gameday.** Two visual registers and an explicit list of which surfaces get the loud one. Front-office work is quiet and legible; kickoff, the draft clock and a championship are allowed to shout. A screen that is loud for no reason is a bug.

2. **Color is information, never decoration.** The only color carriers are: team identity (accent surfaces), rating tiers, win/loss, cap health, and injury status. Every one of them also carries a non-color cue — a letter, a number, an SF Symbol, a word — so the screen still reads with color vision deficiency or in bright sun.

3. **One concept per card.** If a card needs a scroll of its own or a second heading, it is two cards. Depth lives behind a tap (sheet, push), not in density.

4. **Accessible by construction, not by audit.** Every screen survives Dynamic Type at XXXL without truncation or overlap, 4.5:1 contrast in both themes, 44pt minimum targets, meaningful VoiceOver labels on stat rows and chips (a box score row reads as a sentence, not nine loose numbers), and Reduce Motion honored on every transition and celebration.

5. **Tokens, never literals.** Spacing, radii, type, rating bands and semantic colors are invented once in `DesignSystem.swift` and referenced everywhere else — the same way `LeagueRules.swift` owns gameplay constants. A magic number in a view is a defect.

6. **Say the number, then say what it means.** Copy states the fact and the consequence in one short line, in the player's terms. Show the cap hit *and* the dead money. Show the odds *and* the edge. Never make the player do arithmetic the engine already did.
