# M3 Task 5 — Two-window transfer portal plan

Authority: Master Build Documentation Phase F; the M3 college-management plan; ADR 0004 unified
NIL authority; ADR 0005 two atomic portal windows. This plan is an implementation contract, not a
completion claim.

## Dependency boundary

Portal processing begins only after participant-based appearances and usage-aware redshirt outcomes
are authoritative. Redshirt resolution must precede eligibility advancement so a preserved season
cannot be graduated before portal eligibility is calculated. The unified `ProgrammeNILState` remains
the only money authority.

## State and history

Add an independent `CollegePortalState` to `CollegeState`; recruiting remains `.active` throughout.
Portal phases are `closed`, `postseasonOpen`, `awaitingSpring`, and `springOpen`. State is bound to a
target season and owns at most 300 canonical entries plus at most two window summaries. Redshirt
state already moved the internal root to schema 5, so this required portal state moves it to schema
6; decoding must never synthesize an empty portal for a schema-6 root.

Each entry owns player/source IDs, target season/window, scholarship status, an immutable named
intent explanation, and a bounded canonical offer list. Offers snapshot permitted-knowledge fit and
the portal NIL reservation; they never become a second money authority.

`PlayerCareerRecord` owns at most ten durable portal-window records—two windows across the five-year
eligibility clock. A record retains intent, retention, offers, resolution, source/destination, and
final roster NIL. Transfer preserves the same player, lifecycle, eligibility, recruiting origin,
existing career seasons, and career identity. Current portal entries and durable career records use
one record shape and whole-root integrity requires exact equality, so a UI-facing hot copy cannot
drift from history.

Portal-player observations are observer-scoped in `ScoutingState`, bounded to the national pool,
and cleared for the next target season. Hidden potential must never influence an offer when the
permitted observation and public career record are unchanged.

## Deterministic order

At completed-season final week:

1. finalize competition and participant aggregates;
2. compute redshirt resolution, completed career seasons, eligibility and departures while the
   completed schedule and participant aggregates are still available;
3. archive the completed season and install the next-season schedule before changing any roster;
4. apply departures, clean scholarships and remove departed roster NIL;
5. sign commitments;
6. renew next-season NIL ledgers;
7. open and resolve the postseason portal atomically;
8. add only minimum-position walk-ons, using a postseason-specific deterministic ordinal namespace;
9. renew recruiting and persist next-season week 1 as `awaitingSpring`.

The schedule replacement in step 3 is mandatory: current result manifests are validated against
current roster ownership. Portal intent reads completed usage from the just-appended
`PlayerCareerSeason` and team success from `SeasonArchive.finalCollegeRanking`; it does not retain a
second statistics snapshot or mutate rosters while completed results are still live.

At next-season week 1, `.marketInteractions` requires an `awaitingSpring` root with no result in the
new schedule, opens and resolves spring before the recruiting market or any game, fills the
remaining declared roster target with explicit walk-ons from a distinct spring ordinal namespace,
then closes the portal.

## Resolution transaction

Work on copies in canonical UUID order. Select eligible intent scores above a calibrated threshold,
cap the pool by score then UUID, and let the source make the smallest affordable roster-NIL increase
that crosses its retention threshold. Failed trials leave the ledger byte-identical. Newly signed
players have no completed career season and cannot enter. The `restless` trait becomes live only
when deterministic college-player/prospect generation, signing and walk-on paths all preserve it;
do not fabricate it only inside the intent calculation.

### Frozen policy version 1

Portal facts persist their policy version so later tuning cannot rewrite historical reasons. Version
1 derives observer confidence as
`min(95, 25 + (recruitingStaff - 40) * 20 / 59 + min(20, appearances))` and evidence as
`min(maxEvidence, appearances + max(1, recruitingStaff / 20))`. Rated attributes and potential are
estimated from truth once through a seed scoped by target season, window, observer, source, and
player; all downstream decisions consume only that immutable observation.

The six intent components are canonical and integer-only:

- playing time: `20 - min(20, appearances * 20 / 12)`;
- roster path: `min(15, max(0, positionRoom - 1) * 15 / max(1, positionTarget - 1))`;
- relationship: `10 - score / 5` when the source owns a preserved recruiting relationship,
  otherwise `-min(5, completedSeasonsAtSource)`;
- NIL: `-min(20, sourceRosterNIL / 100)`;
- team success: `(oneBasedFinalRank - 1) * 20 / 133 - 10`;
- restless: `10` only when the stored player trait exists, otherwise `0`.

Totals at least 25 enter, ordered by total descending and player UUID, capped at 300. Retention
prioritises starts, then appearances, then UUID. If the current NIL band is `min(20, NIL / 100)`,
the exact band increase needed is `intentTotal - 25 + 1`; the smallest corresponding allocation is
trialled on a copy. An impossible or unaffordable trial releases the entrant without changing the
ledger. Spring may record a second decision after postseason, but one player cannot transfer twice
for the same target season.

Destination policy remains separate from player preference. A player with a recruiting origin
retains those eight pitch priorities; an initial player or walk-on uses the frozen neutral weight
70 for every pitch. Version 1 records the distance and position-target evidence needed to rederive
fit without consulting hidden truth. Its raw player-preference components are the recruiting
integer forms: proximity (or neutral 5), prestige, playing-time room, observed scheme fit,
relationship 0, staff quality, archived team success, and offered NIL. Each raw value is weighted
by the preserved priority over 99; the direct NIL adjustment is `offer / 100`, while the player-
preference knowledge adjustment is zero. Estimated potential and confidence instead belong to the
school's distinct admission score.

For each destination, fixed openings are `min(rosterOpenings, scholarshipOpenings)` after retention
and before any departure. The school is willing to consider its top five admission-ranked players
per opening; players then shortlist at most five willing destinations using zero-NIL fit. After that
membership is frozen, each school gives every shortlisted offer the same deterministic NIL term:
`min(500, floor(nilRemaining / offerCount / 100) * 100)`. This keeps the sum of every live offer
within the captured ledger, permits zero-dollar offers, and prevents circular reranking from
inventing more budget. Final preferences include those terms. Entrants propose in preference order;
destinations retain the best admission scores up to fixed openings, with UUID tie-breaks. Rejected
entrants continue until their five choices are exhausted. Input order and outbound vacancies never
change the result.

Destinations evaluate candidates through the shared integer destination-fit boundary using only
permitted knowledge. Capture destination roster openings, scholarship openings and available NIL
before any offer or departure, then use deterministic many-to-one deferred acceptance. Outbound
players never create capacity usable during the same window. Accepted moves remove source
ownership, scholarship, and roster NIL; add destination ownership/scholarship; reclassify the exact
portal reservation into roster NIL; and append history/events. Losing reservations refund. The NIL
authority therefore needs atomic roster-allocation set/remove and portal-to-roster reclassification
operations in addition to its existing reservation/refund operations. Unmatched entrants return
unchanged. Commit only if whole-root integrity passes; otherwise discard every copy and emit
nothing.

## History and integrity

Emit typed `portalEntered`, `portalRetentionResolved`, `portalOfferMade`, `playerTransferred`, and
`portalWindowCompleted` events. Hot events must bind exactly to durable career records. Completion
counts are re-derived from those records, so a window remains verifiable after its hot entry
collection is replaced.

Hostile decoding and root integrity reject phase/season disagreement, more than 300 entries,
identity/key mismatch, orphan/ineligible players/programmes, duplicate ownership, source equal to
destination, more than one transfer per target season, offer/reservation mismatch, NIL snapshot
drift, capacity overflow, hidden/future/mismatched observations, malformed career chronology,
summary disagreement, a surviving stable-boundary portal NIL reservation, spring after a
target-season result, and a partial postseason roster without minimum position coverage. This
save-shape change bumps the internal root from schema 5 to 6; the outer save-envelope version remains
an independent persistence concern.

## Implementation checkpoint — policy steps 4–6

The observer-knowledge, intent, live-trait, and retention boundary is implemented under schema 6.
One sealed, non-`Codable` policy snapshot binds the exact target season, portal window, predecessor
phase, calendar, root seed, authoritative entrant evidence, permitted player truth, and observer
staff quality. Knowledge and retention consume that snapshot rather than caller-created intent
objects. Knowledge writes are immutable and batch-canonical, reject mixed slices and empty observer
buckets, and enforce the frozen 134-observer/300-player bounds. Retention is atomic and preserves
the exact `199 -> 200` one-dollar boundary as well as the saturated/unaffordable release cases.

Only `.restless` is currently populated because it is the only trait with a live M3 consumer. Its
draw uses the stable person identity plus the trait's fixed ordinal substream, so later activation
of another trait cannot reshuffle existing saves or player generation. The remaining seven traits
stay registered in FSC-014 rather than appearing as decorative labels.

## Implementation checkpoint — matching step 7

The market resolver is implemented as a second sealed, non-`Codable` snapshot derived directly
from the authoritative root. It owns actual post-retention programme ledgers, fixed pre-departure
capacity, map/rank/roster baselines, player origin priorities, history exclusions, and scouting;
the matcher accepts no caller-created intent, capacity, knowledge, priority, or NIL input.

Version 1 now freezes the complete player-preference and destination-admission formulas. Persisted
offer evidence carries known-or-neutral origin, squared distance, position target and room,
observed scheme fit, eight canonical priority weights, programme quality, archived rank, and exact
knowledge. Destinations admit their top five candidates per fixed opening, entrants shortlist five
willing destinations, and each school offers an equal `0...500` term derived from the number of
actual shortlisted entrants and its captured remaining NIL. Entrant-proposing deferred acceptance
then resolves separate preference/admission orderings with UUID tie-breaks. Same-window outbound
vacancies never expand capacity; losing promises refund exactly; accepted promises alone remain in
the transient result for the later whole-root commit.

Focused tests cover genuine rejection-and-continuation, capacity displacement, zero-dollar and

## Implementation checkpoint — atomic transaction step 8 and integrity step 11

The sealed matching result now commits without accepting a caller-authored root or mutation input.
One pure attempt updates rosters, exact scholarship ownership, the unified NIL ledger, player
careers, stable portal state, scouting knowledge, and the bounded history ledger; any invalid
career, ownership, capacity, money, sequence, or final integrity condition returns no transition
and leaves the captured root byte-identical. Programmes may send and receive in the same batch,
positive and zero-dollar promises retain distinct truthful representations, and spring replaces hot
postseason entries while preserving both summaries and the durable two-window career chain.

Typed portal entry, retention, offer, transfer, and completion events are emitted in canonical
groups with deterministic IDs. Whole-root integrity binds every retained fact to an exact durable
record and calendar, rejects duplicates while allowing a truthfully archived prefix, rederives both
current-target summaries, validates the latest current ownership/scholarship/NIL result, and
regroups historical career offers after live entries rotate out. Current-target observer knowledge
must bind to a durable entrant and exact embedded offer evidence. Canonical indexes keep these
checks linear in career records, offers, observations, and retained events, with an empty-portal
fast path for ordinary weekly worlds.

## Implementation checkpoint — scheduler/cycle steps 9–10 and characterization step 12

Both windows now run in the fixed world scheduler. Final-week processing preserves the completed
participant/career evidence while installing the next schedule before roster movement, then applies
lifecycle and redshirt outcomes, reconciles scholarships, signs the class, renews NIL/recruiting,
commits the sealed postseason market, and adds only minimum-position walk-ons. The saved root advances
to target-season week 1 as `awaitingSpring`. At week 1 the spring market commits before scouting,
recruiting, AI, and games; a distinct deterministic walk-on namespace fills the declared roster
target, then the portal closes. Direct scouting, market, AI, presentation intent, and shared
recruiting-action authorities cannot bypass the spring pause.

Walk-on UUID collision avoidance covers the normalized world namespace, including league structure,
map, schedule, history, pending, people, and archived identities. Existing transaction events retain
their original deterministic IDs even when a portal batch exceeds the hot-ledger limit. Temporary
coverage tolerance applies only to college programmes inside the sealed portal commit.

A two-season replay exposed and fixed one real interconnection defect: flat portal capacity could
consume an opening already reserved for minimum positional coverage. Frozen V1 capacity evidence now
persists per-position deficits, deferred acceptance enforces
`accepted + remainingDeficit <= rosterOpenings`, and durable/root checks revalidate the accepted
position set. No player is silently cut and no repair may create a 106-player roster.
