## 1. Why this file exists

Every per-phase gate in `docs/05-IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md` requires touched surfaces to audit
**≥17/20 with zero P0/P1** "against the same rubric that produced the 9/20 baseline". That rubric
lived only inside a tool. A gate that cites an unwritten standard is not a gate — it is a hope. This
file makes the number measurable.

It is also the answer to the audit's own central lesson, `AUDIT.md:783`:

> *"The defect is not ignorance of contrast; it is that the test's coverage boundary became the
> quality boundary."*

A rubric that is written down but not enforced by tests reproduces exactly that failure one level up.
So §7 fixes the procedure, not just the scale.

## 3. The five dimensions

The audit ran **five dimension finders in parallel** (`AUDIT.md:7`). A finding belongs to exactly one
dimension, declared in its `Category` field — though the *same underlying defect* is legitimately
filed twice when two finders reach it by different routes (the 21pt chip targets appear as
Accessibility at `AUDIT.md:138` and Platform Conformance at `AUDIT.md:246`; the sub-44pt
`.controlSize(.small)` buttons appear at `AUDIT.md:365` and `AUDIT.md:563`). Duplicate filings score
against both dimensions. That is deliberate: a target that is too small is both an accessibility
failure and a platform failure, and fixing it should credit both.

| Dimension | What it grades |
|---|---|
| **Accessibility** | Whether a user with a disability can complete the same tasks as anyone else: VoiceOver reachability and utterance quality, Dynamic Type survival, contrast as experienced, Reduce Motion, touch-target floors, never-colour-alone |
| **Performance** | Whether the app stays responsive on the paths the user actually takes: main-thread blocking, work in `body`, observation scope, I/O, allocation churn, growth curves over a decade-long save |
| **Appearance & Theming** | Whether the design system is real: token discipline, measured colour against composited surfaces in both themes, register consistency, no literals in views |
| **Platform Conformance** | Whether it behaves like an iPhone app: stock controls, navigation and modality conventions, nav-bar slot semantics, SF Symbols, error presentation, HIG-specified affordances |
| **Adaptivity** | Whether it survives the range of the single supported device class: orientation policy, smallest device × largest type, safe-area ownership, keyboard, text-input configuration |

## 4. The 0–4 scale

### 4.1 Generic band semantics

These apply to every dimension. Per-dimension anchors in §5 instantiate them.

| Score | Band | Operative test |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Absent | No system exists for this dimension. The defect is the default and conformance is the accident. The most common platform failure mode for this dimension is present. |
| **1** | Written but unbuilt | A commitment exists in the project's own documents and has **essentially zero implementation**: at least one required practice occurs **zero times** across the whole layer, and at least one complete user-facing surface is unusable on this dimension. |
| **2** | Real system, systemic breach | A deliberate system exists and holds across most of the code, but violations recur **at scale** (tens of call sites) and include at least one P0 or P1. The breaches trace to a **missing rule or a missing test**, not to carelessness. |
| **3** | Clean by review | No P0 or P1 remains on this dimension. Remaining findings are P2/P3 and are **isolated outliers** against a house style the code otherwise holds. The rule is written down; conformance is enforced by human review. |
| **4** | Machine-verified | The rule is asserted by an automated test whose **coverage boundary equals the surface boundary** — every surface the rule applies to is enumerated by the test, and the test reads the **shipped** values rather than a copy of them. A new surface fails by default instead of passing silently. |

The 4/4 anchor is not invented. It is the shape of the one thing the baseline codebase already did
right, described at `AUDIT.md:804` and `AUDIT.md:838`: `DesignSystemTests` checks all five rating
tiers against the card, the page **and** the composited 14% chip wash in both themes, and
`runTeamTintTests` does the same per-team for all 32 shipped tints, "reading the shipped hexes rather
than a copy". The audit calls that "better colour discipline than most shipping apps". The dimension
still scored 2/4 because **that same discipline covered one system out of many** — which is precisely
the 4-versus-2 distinction.

### 4.2 Why 1/4 and not 2/4, in the one case it happened

Accessibility was the only dimension at 1/4. The distinguishing evidence is *zero occurrences*, not
*many violations*:

- Reduce Motion: **0 occurrences in the entire UI layer** (`AUDIT.md:84–91`, `AUDIT.md:789`), against
  an explicit written commitment in both `PRODUCT.md` and `DESIGN.md`.
- VoiceOver: **3 accessibility modifiers in ~140 KB of view code, 0 `accessibilityElement` calls**
  (`AUDIT.md:789–791`).
- A whole advertised game mode collapses: the On-the-Field aiming surface is an unlabeled
  `DragGesture` on a `.contentShape(Rectangle())`, so "a VoiceOver user's only operable action during
  a live snap is 'Hand Off' … an entire advertised game mode collapses to one button"
  (`AUDIT.md:120–127`).

The other four dimensions all had a real, working system underneath their violations. Accessibility
did not. **1/4 is "the practice does not exist here", 2/4 is "the practice exists and leaks".**

## 6. Severity: P0–P3

### 6.1 Definitions, as operated

Extracted from how 78 findings were actually classified. The severity is a function of three things,
and the audit trades them off rather than applying any one mechanically:

1. **Recoverability** of the consequence — can the user get back what they lost?
2. **Reachability** — is the defect on a path the user cannot avoid, or one they may never take?
3. **Blast radius and distance from threshold** — how many call sites, and how far past the stated
   limit.

| Severity | Count | Operative definition | Decisive evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P0 — Blocking** | 1 | The defect sits on a path **every** user action takes, violates a **stated numeric commitment** by a **measured** margin, and has no workaround. P0 is reserved: one finding in 78 earned it. | `AUDIT.md:73` — `mutate` funnels every state change into a 2.4–3.3 MB synchronous encode + backup copy + atomic write + directory rescan on the main actor at 11 sites. Measured 84 ms at week 11, 112 ms at the playoffs; a "Sim Week" measured 160 ms of disk I/O on top of 105 ms of engine work, against `PRODUCT.md`'s "week advance under 150 ms". `advanceOffseasonStage` pays it 2–3 times, `completeDraftStage` up to 5. ### 6.2 The P1/P2 boundary, stated plainly

The same class of defect lands on both sides of it, so the boundary is worth naming precisely.

Touch targets: **21pt is P1** (`AUDIT.md:138`, `246`), **28pt is P2** (`AUDIT.md:365`, `563`). Both
are irreversible actions in a scrolling list. The difference is distance from the threshold — the
audit's words are "under half the 44pt minimum" — compounded by adjacency: the P1 case has three
irreversible point-spending actions 6pt apart.

Modality: **an irreversible action in the cancellation slot is P1** (`AUDIT.md:237`); **a sheet with
no dismiss control is P2** (`AUDIT.md:482`); **a "Done" label in the cancellation slot is P2**
(`AUDIT.md:491`). The P1 destroys the user's game; the P2s cost time and confidence.

**The rule:** P1 when the cost is unrecoverable or the task becomes impossible; P2 when the cost is
time, confidence or hierarchy.

### 6.3 The raise/refute discipline — how 84 became 78

`AUDIT.md:7–9`: every finding was re-opened **at its cited line by an adversarial verifier instructed
to refute it**, and the six refutations are kept (`AUDIT.md:900–927`) "because what the audit got
wrong is as useful as what it found".

The refutation criterion is stated inside the appendix at `AUDIT.md:914`:

> *"speculation about a hypothetical … with no user-visible consequence, which is exactly the
> refutation criterion"*

A finding is **refuted, not downgraded**, when it fails any of these:

- **It must cite a line, and the line must say what the finding says it says.** Two refutations turned
  on API semantics the finder assumed rather than read: `.background(.bar)` defaults to
  `ignoresSafeAreaEdges: .all` (`AUDIT.md:920`); a single-line SwiftUI `TextField` resigns first
  responder on Return with no `onSubmit` (`AUDIT.md:908`).
- **It must demonstrate a user-visible consequence.** `AUDIT.md:912` — work in `init` that the first
  frame consumes is a prerequisite, not overhead; measured at 0.28 ms.
- **It must not hedge.** `AUDIT.md:904` — "The finding hedges between two opposite runtime behaviours
  and verifies neither."
- **It must be a deviation from the system, not the system.** `AUDIT.md:916` — the finding's premise
  was what `DESIGN.md` prescribes.
- **Code that cannot render is not a defect on a user-facing dimension.** `AUDIT.md:924` — though
  dead code is separately worth deleting (Pattern 5, `AUDIT.md:796`).

Reproducing the baseline's rigour requires reproducing this step. **An audit that has not been
adversarially verified has not run.**

### 6.4 The finding schema

Every finding in the baseline carries these fields. A reconstructed audit that omits them is not
comparable to the 9/20 number:

```
**<One-sentence claim, with the measured number in it>**
- **Location**: `<path>:<line>` — N sites
- **Category**: <one of the five dimensions>
- **Impact**: <what the user experiences, with measurements>
- **Guideline**: <the doc line or HIG section broken — or literally "none">
- **Recommendation**: <the concrete fix, at the call site>
- **Suggested command**: <the follow-up tool invocation>
```

Two rules embedded in that schema are load-bearing:

- **Measurements, not adjectives.** Impacts carry numbers: `84 ms`, `2.77:1`, `21pt`, `349 ms`,
  `126 sites`. A finding with no number is weak and probably P3.
- **`Guideline: none` is a legal value** and is used honestly — `AUDIT.md:161`, `179`, `388` all say
  "none (no DESIGN.md or HIG rule; this is a straightforward … defect)". A finding must either name
  the rule it breaks or admit that it is asserting a new one.

### 6.5 Positive findings are part of the output

`AUDIT.md:800–882` lists ~40 positive findings at the same level of specificity as the defects. They
are what separates 2/4 from 1/4 in four of five dimensions — a dimension's score is not `4 - defects`,
it is a judgement about whether a working system exists underneath. **An audit run that reports only
defects cannot produce a comparable score.** Positive findings also protect correct code from being
"fixed": `AUDIT.md:826` explicitly says `SaveStore.list()` decoding only the meta sidecars "is exactly
the right shape and should be preserved when the rest of persistence moves off the main actor".

## 7. Scoring procedure

1. **Fix the surface set.** Name every file in scope. For a phase gate, that is the files the phase
   touched, plus — critically — every screen that consumes a shared token file the phase edited. A
   change to `DesignSystem.swift` touches all of them (Pattern 1, `AUDIT.md:779`).
2. **Run five dimension passes** over that surface set, one per dimension, independently.
3. **Adversarially verify** every raised finding at its cited line, instructed to refute (§6.3).
   Record refutations.
4. **Classify** each confirmed finding P0–P3 per §6.
5. **Collect positive findings** per §6.5.
6. **Score each dimension 0–4** against §5, using the count and severity of confirmed findings and
   the presence or absence of a working system.
7. **Extract systemic patterns** — the baseline's five (`AUDIT.md:777–798`) are worth more than its
   78 findings, and the spec brief makes each one a standing invariant with a named test in
   `docs/06-AUDIT-DISPOSITION.md`.
8. **Sum, and apply the band** from §8.2.

## 8. Global versus local dimensions

### 8.1 The problem this solves

A phase that touches three views cannot be graded on an app-wide orientation policy. The baseline's
own evidence makes this concrete: the orientation finding is located at `App/project.yml:22` — a
build-configuration file, not a view — and the audit calls it "the root cause of four other findings
in this report" (`AUDIT.md:268`). No view-level phase can create it, fix it, or be blamed for its
absence. The same holds for navigation structure and large-title policy: `AUDIT.md:473` spans **20
sites across the whole app** and its fix is a policy decision ("Keep `.large` for the five tab roots
only"), not a per-view edit.

### 8.2 The split

| Dimension | Scope | Scored at |
|---|---|---|
| Accessibility | **LOCAL** | Every phase gate, on touched surfaces |
| Performance | **LOCAL** | Every phase gate, on touched surfaces |
| Appearance & Theming | **LOCAL** | Every phase gate, on touched surfaces |
| Platform Conformance | **GLOBAL** | Milestone boundaries only |
| Adaptivity | **GLOBAL** | Milestone boundaries only |

The two global dimensions still contribute **local pass/fail checks** at every phase gate. They are
not scored per phase; they are not ignored per phase.

**Platform Conformance — GLOBAL checks (milestone):**

- Navigation structure: tab count and identity, one `NavigationStack` per tab, no hand-rolled global
  navigation (`AUDIT.md:26–30`, `866`)
- Large-title policy: `.large` at tab roots only, `.inline` on every pushed detail screen and every
  modal root (`AUDIT.md:473`)
- One app-wide tint established at the root, not overridden per-CTA (`AUDIT.md:545`, `862`)
- A Settings surface exists and exposes the Light/Dark/System control the design system specifies
  (`AUDIT.md:41–43`, `554`)
- A single error-presentation policy: no failure path writes to a property no view reads
  (`AUDIT.md:255`)

**Platform Conformance — LOCAL checks (per phase, pass/fail):**

- Every sheet/`fullScreenCover` the phase adds has a visible dismiss (`AUDIT.md:482`)
- Cancellation slot leading, confirmation slot trailing; no irreversible action in the cancellation
  slot (`AUDIT.md:237`, `491`)
- Stock controls; nothing hand-rolled that a system style provides (`AUDIT.md:509`)
- Every control ≥44pt; no `.controlSize(.small)` on a primary action (`AUDIT.md:563`)
- SF Symbols only; no `.font(.system(size:))` (`AUDIT.md:500`, `581`)

**Adaptivity — GLOBAL checks (milestone):**

- An orientation policy is declared **and enforced in code** (`AUDIT.md:264`). The app is
  **landscape-only** (owner, 2026-08-10), so the policy is still trivially stateable, and
  `AUDIT.md:270`'s argument carries over unchanged with the orientations swapped: declaring one
  orientation "removes the [other-orientation] failure modes from the other 20 screens in one change
  rather than making each of them [other-orientation]-safe". What the baseline punished was the
  *absence* of a policy, not the choice of value.
- **Both landscape size classes render.** Compact-width (standard, SE) and regular-width (Plus, Max)
  are now distinct cases, per §2's note. A two-pane layout that only survives on a Max is a P1.
- The safe-area ownership convention for bottom chrome — `safeAreaInset(edge:)`, not a stacked child
  (`AUDIT.md:590`, 11 sites). The *convention* is set once at milestone level; its *application* is a
  local check thereafter.
- The full-bleed policy for hero bands (`AUDIT.md:759`). Same shape: decided once, applied per phase.

**Adaptivity — LOCAL checks (per phase, pass/fail):**

- Every touched screen survives the **844 × 390 landscape design floor** at AX5 without truncation or overlap (`AUDIT.md:282`, `291`; floor narrowed by the owner 2026-08-10, `04` §4.1). At 667 × 375 — unsupported but installable — the weaker assertion applies: reachable, not beautiful
- No literal `.frame(width:)` or `.frame(height:)` around scaling text (`AUDIT.md:93`, `599`)
- Non-scrolling columns are wrapped or made proportional (`AUDIT.md:273`, `282`)
- Text fields declare `textContentType`, capitalisation and autocorrect (`AUDIT.md:608`)

### 8.3 How a phase gate reaches a number out of 20

Only three dimensions are scored at a phase gate, but the gate is stated as ≥17/**20**. The rule:

> A phase gate scores the three local dimensions on touched surfaces and **inherits** the two global
> dimensions' scores from the most recent milestone measurement. The inherited score is **capped at 2**
> for any global dimension whose local checks the phase failed.

Consequences the plan's authors must accept:

- **Global policy must be set before the first UI phase closes.** With no prior milestone measurement,
  the inherited score is 0 and no phase can reach 17. So the first UI phase — or a Phase 0 that
  precedes it — establishes orientation policy, navigation structure, large-title policy, root tint,
  error presentation and the Settings surface, and takes the first global measurement. This is also
  the cheapest possible ordering: `AUDIT.md:268` shows one missing policy generating four downstream
  findings.
- **A global regression is visible immediately.** Failing a local check of a global dimension caps
  that dimension at 2, which costs ≥2 points and usually fails the gate. That is the intended
  pressure.
- **`docs/05-IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md` must name its milestone boundaries explicitly.** A rubric cannot
  define them. Absent an explicit list, the global gate runs at every phase boundary and the
  "deferred to milestone" concession does not apply.

| Total | Band label |
|---|---|
| 18–20 | Excellent — ship |
| 15–17 | Good — targeted fixes |
| 12–14 | Fair — systemic gaps |
| **8–11** | **Poor — major overhaul** |
| 0–7 | Unshippable — rebuild the layer |

Only the **8–11 → "Poor — major overhaul"** row is evidenced: `AUDIT.md:20` and `AUDIT.md:47` attach

acceptable to ship a phase from.

## 9. What ≥17/20 actually commits the project to

Stated plainly, because the number is easy to write and expensive to hit.

**The arithmetic.** 20 − 17 = **3 points of headroom across 5 dimensions**, with each dimension capped
at 4.

- If **at most one** dimension is at 4/4, then four dimensions are at ≤3, which loses at least 4
  points, giving a total of at most 16.
- Therefore **≥17 requires at least two dimensions at a perfect 4/4**, on every gate.

The complete set of shapes that reach exactly 17: `{4,4,3,3,3}`, `{4,4,4,3,2}`, `{4,4,4,4,1}`. The
first is the realistic one. Note also that **no dimension may sit at 1/4** unless the other four are
all perfect, and **no dimension may sit at 0/4 at all**.

**The baseline for comparison.** The 9/20 audit's *best* dimension was **2/4**, and the mean was
**1.8/4**. Nothing in `Sources/ProFootballCoachUI/` scored 3 or 4 on anything. So ≥17 is not an
incremental improvement on the previous build's craft — it is:

- **at least +2** on two dimensions, from the best score the project has ever achieved, and
- **at least +1** on the remaining three,
- **on every phase, from the first one.**

**And per §4.1, 4/4 is not "clean on inspection" — it is machine-verified.** A dimension reaches 4
only when an automated test enumerates every surface the rule applies to and reads the shipped values.
So the real commitment behind ≥17/20 is:

> **On every phase gate, at least two dimensions must be carrying an automated test whose coverage
> boundary equals the touched-surface boundary.**

That is the direct answer to Pattern 1 (`AUDIT.md:783`), and it is why the number is 17 rather than
14.

**Which two dimensions.** The plan should name them rather than discovering them per phase. The two
cheapest to machine-verify without a simulator — which matters under D11, since the build environment
has no `swift` and no `xcodebuild` — are:

1. **Appearance & Theming.** A contrast loop over shipped token values is pure arithmetic on hex
   strings. The baseline already had a working one (`AUDIT.md:838`); it simply did not enumerate
   enough surfaces.
2. **Accessibility**, in its testable half: a source-scanning test for Reduce Motion at every
   animation site, for literal frames around scaling text, for hard-coded point sizes, and for a
   label+value on every custom-drawn control. The utterance-quality half (does a row read as a
   sentence?) stays review-enforced and therefore caps at 3 unless composed labels are asserted
   structurally.

**Performance** can reach 4 if D4's budgets get a headless harness. **Adaptivity** probably cannot
reach 4 without a simulator (§5.5), so **do not budget it as one of the two 4/4 dimensions**.
**Platform Conformance** can reach 4 through source-scanning tests, but only at milestone gates.

**If ≥17 proves unreachable in practice, that is a finding, not a failure to hide.** Escalate it as a
blocking owner question — lower the gate deliberately, or fund the tests that make 4/4 reachable. Do
not quietly redefine the surface set until the number passes; that is Pattern 1 happening again, with
the rubric in the role of the test.

## 10. What this rubric does not cover

- **The engine.** `AUDIT.md:4` excludes `FootballSimCore`. Engine phases are gated by calibration
  bands, cross-process determinism and the soak — see `docs/03-MATCH-ENGINE.md` — not by this rubric.
- **The two Tier A legal tests** (name collision, trade dress). Separate gate, separate tests.
- **iPad and size classes.** Out of scope in the baseline (`AUDIT.md:5`) and out of scope under P1.
- **App Store review.** Out of scope in the baseline; `docs/PRE-DEPLOYMENT-CHECKLIST.md` owns it.
- **The SwiftUI `Canvas` match view**, which did not exist when the baseline ran. Its nearest analogue
  — the arcade layer — is named the weakest area in the codebase (Pattern 5, `AUDIT.md:796`) and
  supplies P1 findings on four of the five dimensions (`AUDIT.md:120`, `228`, `237`, `273`, `282`,
  `383`). Treat it as the surface most likely to fail a gate, and specify its accessibility and
  Reduce Motion behaviour in D12 **before** it is built, not after.
- **Engagement.** `AUDIT.md` measured craft. The spec brief §2 is explicit: 9/20 is evidence about
  craft, not the diagnosis of blandness. **A phase can score 20/20 and still be boring.** Nothing in
  this file speaks to whether the game is fun; `docs/01-RESEARCH.md` §6.0 and `docs/02-GAME-DESIGN.md`
  own that.
