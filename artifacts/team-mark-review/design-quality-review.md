# Team Mark Design & Quality Review

Scope: 166 shipped marks. This combines the subject-repetition audit with a visual review of the production canvas and recorded contrast checks.

## Verdict

The marks share a usable baseline — flat vector construction, transparent backgrounds, two-to-three colour palettes, and a dark keyline. Individual craft is generally serviceable. As a **pack**, however, it is only moderately consistent: six composition systems compete, repeated templates make many identities interchangeable, and 64 marks have a recorded dark-surface silhouette failure.

| Review area | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Core production style | Flat colour, hard edges, no typography, transparent assets, dark keyline | Consistent |
| Subject variety | 140 of 166 marks belong to 48 repeated motifs | Needs rebalancing |
| Family language | Profiles, animal illustrations, object icons, landscapes, framed crests, and motion graphics follow different composition rules | Needs alignment |
| Small-size recognition | Dense framing and thin/crossed elements risk collapse at 20pt | Needs simplification in affected families |
| Dark-surface separation | 64 recorded `Outstanding` contrast exceptions | Must fix |

## Repetition: the main identity problem

The highest-repeat motifs are palisades (7), shield bosses (7), beacon braziers (6), fletched arrows (6), and harriers (5). The broad groups are tools/trade implements (32), birds (28), human heads (27), defence/ship structures (23), and landscape/local markers (23).

The issue is not just repeated nicknames. Each repeat uses closely related composition as well, so colour is often the only meaningful differentiator.

## Quality and consistency findings

1. **The character family is visually strong but overused.** It has 27 head-in-profile marks, with recurring hats, hoods, helmets, and face angles. Several are good individual sports marks; together they read as one long run of near-identical portrait badges.
2. **The animal family has the best potential variety but is bird-heavy.** Birds account for 28 marks while mammals have 12 and mythic creatures 3. Several birds vary only by beak, feather colour, or wing pose.
3. **Objects are clearer than they are distinctive.** Anchors, compasses, beacons, shields, plates, and gun ports are clean at a glance but repeat a generic icon grammar. They need more subject diversity, not more colour variation.
4. **Framed emblems and abstract-motion marks break the pack’s hierarchy.** Frames introduce a separate crest system, while motion marks rely on thin diagonals, crossings, and internal lines. Both become visually busier than the profile and object marks at small sizes.
5. **Regional symbols are the most promising source of uniqueness.** Their place-based forms are less repetitive, but their proportions range from compact icons to narrow scenes. Simplify them to one dominant silhouette before using that direction more broadly.
6. **Contrast is a production issue, not an aesthetic preference.** 64 assets are explicitly recorded as needing a keyline or halo to retain their outer silhouette on a dark surface. This affects every existing family, led by animals (15), characters (13), abstract motion (12), and framed emblems (10).

## Recommended order of work

1. Apply a consistent dark-surface keyline/halo treatment to the 64 recorded exceptions; verify at 20, 32, and 44 points.
2. Replace the repeated human-head, bird, tool, and fortification motifs before touching the stronger one-off marks.
3. Keep the baseline style rules: transparent square canvas, one dominant silhouette, two or three flat colours, consistent keyline, and no text.
4. Build new subject families around fish/aquatic life, insects, plants, weather, geology, and regional ecology. Give each a distinct silhouette grammar, not a new version of a crest or profile head.
5. Use framed emblems sparingly as an intentional exception, and simplify abstract-motion marks so no identifying feature depends on thin crossed strokes.

## Decision rule for remakes

Mark an asset for replacement when two or more apply:

- its exact motif appears three or more times;
- it belongs to the overfull character, bird, tool, or fortification clusters;
- it has a recorded dark-surface contrast exception;
- its recognition relies on a frame, thin internal lines, or several crossed elements at 20pt.

This preserves the sound core of the system while focusing remake effort where it materially improves both differentiation and production quality.
