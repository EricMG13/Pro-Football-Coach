The Inbox holds one item, and that is the honest form
One card fills 303 pt at AX5. The rail-plus-detail split has nowhere to go, so it collapses into a queue of one — which is the honest shape of a screen whose job is "answer the next thing". The cost line survives because it is the shortest string on the card and was written to be. Copy length is a Dynamic Type constraint, not a style preference.
The commit pins, and this render is why the rule exists
Two option cards cannot sit side by side at 34 pt, so the pane is one column and SegmentedControl is a list per §3's above-AX3 rule. If the commit scrolled with the content it would sit below the fold and SmallestDeviceLayoutTest would fail on the most important control on the screen. It is pinned at 44 pt above the bar.
The bar does not scale, and that is deliberate
The destination bar stays 44 pt with 12.5 pt labels at every type size. It is the one place the system refuses Dynamic Type, because a bar that grew to 53 pt would take 140 pt of a 303 pt budget and leave less than two rows. This is a real accessibility trade and it must be a stated exemption in §6, not a silent one — the labels are redundant with icons and the destinations are reachable by VoiceOver regardless.

WHAT THIS GROUP SENDS BACK TO 04
A canvas is never narrated
One rule for both canvases: hidden from VoiceOver, with a named text twin carrying the meaning. Converts §6's hardest clause into something testable.
The bar is a stated Dynamic Type exemption
It stays 44 pt at AX5. A scaling bar would eat 140 pt of 303. This is a real trade and §6 must name it as an exemption rather than let it look like an oversight.
Copy length is a Dynamic Type constraint
The cost line survives AX5 because it was written short. §7 should say so — a copy rule with a layout consequence, not a style preference.
Targets are regions, not ink
A 24 pt chip carries a 44 pt region. TouchTargetTest asserts the region; the prior build's four 21 pt chip failures were ink measured as target.
