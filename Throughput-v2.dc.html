Working a roster of 105
The six-lens review's largest single finding: across fourteen files and a twenty-entry registry there was not one filter, sort, search or multi-select, and the player card showed no attributes at all. Both are things Football Manager solved decades ago and this library had not learned. Phase B of docs/plans/2026-08-10-reference-uplift.md; lifts Flexibility and Efficiency from 2.
Rosters are empty id lists until P7 and P8 fill them, so every player here is synthetic. The controls are the deliverable, not the data.

Four controls, and the header holds all of them
Position filter as Chips (already in the registry, no new primitive). Sort as a two-state chip carrying its direction glyph. Search in the detail pane rather than the header, because 785 pt of header is already spent. Multi-select via a leading checkbox that only appears once one row is held. "11 of 105" is the count the filter produced, and it is the single most useful number on the screen.
The detail pane becomes the batch pane
With nothing selected it is the player card. With two or more it is the batch actions, and the actions state their consequence in the label — "Release both, 2 scholarships back" rather than "Release". That is §3's destructive rule applied to a plural, and it answers the review's finding that two drawn verdicts recommended batch operations the UI could not perform.
At AX5 the controls become a sheet
Five chips at 53 pt do not fit 785 pt. Stated reduction: above AX3 the filter row collapses to a single Filter and sort button opening a sheet, and the count moves under the title. Same pattern as SegmentedControl's above-AX3 rule — one behaviour, not two.

The tinted value row is FM's grammar, not its look
FM fills the row behind the label proportionally to the value and bands the numeral. What transfers is the idea that the bar and the number are the same object, so a column of attributes scans as a shape before it is read. What does not transfer is FM's density — it fits 40 attributes in three columns on a desktop; this card carries ten in two, because 44 pt rows and 303 pt of height say so.
The ladder fill reuses §2.1, including the polarity flip
Each row's fill is the rating.* step for its band, and the label sits on the fill — so text polarity flips at the ladder's midpoint exactly as §2.1 now requires. Ball skills 54 and Durability 47 carry near-black; everything above carries white. One rule, applied without exception, and it is visible on this card.
This is a light screen, deliberately
Light ships (owner, 2026-08-10). The densest desk screen in the product is the right place to test it, because if the light appearance survives ten tinted rows and a four-bar chart it survives everything else. Note what changed: the bar track goes #E4E8EE, elevation flips to shadow, the home indicator inverts. The ladder fills are unchanged — §2.1's light ladder was always the light one.

Five chips become one button
Five filter and sort chips at 53 pt need 900 pt of a 785 pt header. Above AX3 they collapse to one 44 pt "Filter and sort" opening a sheet, and the active filter state moves to a line under the title so it is still visible without being interactive.
Three rows, and the glyphs go
303 pt at 62 pt rows is three. The status glyphs are dropped from the row at AX5 — they are 12 pt marks that would need to be 44 pt targets to survive, and the detail pane carries them instead. A stated reduction, not a squeeze.
Multi-select survives
The checkbox is 44 pt at every type size, because it is a target rather than text. This is the screen where Alex most needs batch, and AX5 is where a lesser design would drop it.
