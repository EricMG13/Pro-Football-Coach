The draft is a match view wearing different content
A clock counting down, a queue of events arriving whether you act or not, a decision with a deadline, and a named proposal from a coordinator. That is the call-in loop exactly — so the draft takes the broadcast register, the ScoreBug's live variant becomes the on-the-clock bar, and the recommendation is a CallInCard. Treating it as a list screen with a timer bolted on would build the same interaction twice and get it worse the second time.
224 picks, and the player sees three
Seven rounds of 32 is a long event. The directed-attention rule from §5.2 applies unchanged: the left rail holds the last three picks at decreasing opacity, and the pane holds exactly three options — need, best available, trade down. Everything else is a tap away, not on screen.
Open: what happens when the clock runs out
Auto-pick on the board's top need is the obvious default and it must be stated, because an expired clock that does nothing is a soft-locked draft. It is also the honest answer for a player who put the phone down — the product is a commute game.
