PRO FOOTBALL COACH · DESIGN REFERENCE v2 · GROUP: TOKENS
Broadcast — the token sheet
v1 was a productivity app wearing football's copy: eight of its ten most-used colours were neutral greys and its accent ranked eleventh by usage. This version commits to one world — the television broadcast of a football game — and makes the generated team identity the chrome rather than a token nobody spends. Every ratio is computed against the surface it composites on and was re-derived independently before publication.
Reference read: 19 owner-supplied Football Manager captures. What transferred is the grammar — team-coloured hard-edged furniture, banded numerals, verdict pills, numbered discs. No FM asset, layout, colour or text is reproduced.
All identities synthetic. No real school, franchise, player or colour pair appears.
1  The idiom this world is built on
why a score bug and not a StatCell
Football has exactly one piece of visual furniture that every viewer on earth reads without instruction: the score bug. It is hard-edged, it is segmented, each team's segment carries that team's colour, and the numerals are condensed and tabular so they never reflow. It is the only football-native layout primitive that survives at 390 pt of height — and v1 rendered it as a grey card with 17 pt body text.

Remove every word and it is still a football game in the fourth quarter with two key moments left. Five rules do that work, and they are the whole visual world.
01  ZERO RADIUS
Broadcast furniture is cut, not rounded. The score bug, the LowerThird and the live badge take radius 0. Management cards keep m 12. Radius now separates the game from the desk.
02  TEAM OWNS CHROME
Each side's segment is filled with its own generated primary, at full bleed. This is the only place in the product where the per-save identity becomes visible rather than notional.
03  TABULAR CONDENSED
Score and clock are numeral, a new role: SF Compact, condensed, tabular-nums. A clock that reflows as it counts is the tell of a spreadsheet.
04  BLACK CENTRE
The score itself sits on pure black between the two team fields. It is neutral ground, it guarantees contrast against any generated pair, and it is what makes the bug legible over a bright field.
05  PIPS, NOT A BAR
Remaining key moments are discrete squares, filling in live. 04 §5 requires the player to feel a known-length sequence; a percentage bar cannot say "two moments left".

The fourth card is the open finding, restated because v2 depends on it more than v1 did. All 32 pro TeamTable pairs are dark-primary-with-light-secondary, so the light-primary case is synthetic — it does not occur in the generated data. v1 could shrug at that because team colour touched almost nothing. In v2 team colour fills the score bug on every screen, so a generator that cannot produce a light primary means a product where every programme's chrome is dark and the identity system is narrower than it looks. This is now a generation question with a visible consequence, not a theoretical gap.

Why numeral earns a seventh role. A football product shows a running clock, a score, a down-and-distance and a rating on nearly every surface. In a proportional face those reflow every tick and every roster row sits on a ragged right edge. Tabular condensed fixes both, and it is the single change that most makes the product read as a sport rather than a table. It maps to a real text style so Dynamic Type still governs it.
UNVERIFIED, unchanged from v1. Every AX5 point size here is cited from memory, as is the 44 pt touch floor. Confirm against the HIG type table before 04 §2.2 records them. SF Compact's availability as a Dynamic Type face is a further assumption — if it is not, numeral falls back to .title1 with monospacedDigit(), which keeps the tabular property and loses the condensing.
5  The rating ladder, in two appearances
v1's CVD study survives; it needed a dark twin
v1 tested three ladders under deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia and found only a single-hue lightness ramp keeps five ordered steps. That finding stands and is not re-litigated. What v1 did not supply is a dark-appearance ladder — its light ramp bottoms out at #C2D4E8, which on a near-black page is a glare. Both are below, both monotonic in L*.

The mid-step defect generalises, and that is the finding. In the dark ladder average #2166A8 measures 5.95:1 against white and 3.16:1 against near-black — white works. But the light ladder's average #2E7BC4 clears 4.5:1 against neither white (4.42) nor near-black (4.24). A five-step lightness ramp always has a middle step near L* 50, and L* 50 is exactly where neither polarity of text is safe. So the rule is not "patch the light ladder": a numeral-bearing chip may not use the ladder's mid step at all — it uses the darkened #1F6099 (6.58:1 on white), and the pure ladder stays fill-only as measured. Two variants, one rule, and it is a rule about lightness ramps in general rather than about these ten hexes.
NOW IN 04 — WRITTEN, NOT PROPOSED
Two new roles
live in §2.1 and numeral in §2.2. Both are additions to the role list, so §3's registry and the contrast suite's enumeration both grow. live has no light text form and is a fill there — the same two-mechanism shape elevation already has.
Radius carries meaning
§2.3 assigns radius by component. v2 assigns it by register: 0 for broadcast furniture, m for the desk. A rounded score bug is as wrong as a square inbox row, and neither is currently expressible.
A ladder per appearance
§2.1 records one rating.* ladder. It needs two, like every other colour role, and the mid-step rule stated as a property of lightness ramps rather than as a patch to one hex.
