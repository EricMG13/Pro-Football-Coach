The deuteranopia row is the finding: average and poor converge to ΔE 2 — one colour, not two. Tritanopia puts elite and good at ΔE 3. The glyph column is what keeps the ladder readable at all, which means the colour is decorative here and the glyph is the data — an honest outcome, but it spends the one glyph slot a roster row can least afford (see the Roster card's three-glyph budget).
RETURNED TO OPEN QUESTION 4
The ladder holds five steps, and the way it holds them is lightness. Candidate B keeps five ordered, distinguishable steps under all three deficiencies; A and C do not, and C fails hardest exactly where the brief feared — two of its five steps become one under deuteranopia. Recommendation: adopt B as the ladder, as a fill-only token, and retain C's glyph column as the separate second channel 04 §2.1 already requires for state.*. It does not need to drop to four.
Consequence to write into 04 §2.1 alongside the values: rating.* is a fill role and is not legal as a text colour, so the contrast suite must enumerate it against the surface it fills rather than as a foreground pair. That changes what ContrastByConstructionTest counts.

WHAT THIS SHEET SENDS BACK TO 04
Four things that are not amendments to a value but amendments to a rule, and each is cheaper to settle here than in ContrastByConstructionTest: rating.* is a fill role and cannot be enumerated as a foreground pair; content.tertiary is large-text-and-indicator only in light, and is illegal on surface.raised in dark; every surface seam measures ~1.11:1, so the hairline is a token and not a style; and elevation has two definitions, shadow in light and surface in dark, which means the elevation token cannot be a single shadow value.
