The first run — the hour that sells the game
The v2 review found that the app had no specified entry point. Every screen in 04 §4 assumed a coach already in post, mid-season, with a team — while 02 §10 requires the first fifteen minutes to end with the player having chosen a job with visible stakes, met a stakeholder, set a plan and seen a consequence. Choosing a job is a screen. None of them existed. And 02 §9 is explicit that "what sells the game is the first hour, which is a college hour" — so this was the least-specified part of the most important surface.
All identities synthetic. Programme names, coach names and stakeholder voices are generator-shaped placeholders; no real institution, person or colour pair appears.

One save, and the title screen says so
CLAUDE.md and PRODUCT.md both promise one save, one coach, one continuous career. So there is no slot picker — there is a Continue card carrying the career's actual state, and starting a new one is the destructive act. That framing is the product's positioning rendered as a screen, and it costs one card.
Start a new career is destructive and must behave like it
With one save, "new career" overwrites a career the player may have run for twenty seasons. 04 §3's destructive rule applies in full: confirmed, labelled with what will happen — "This ends the Ashgrove career, year 3, 6–2. It cannot be undone." This is the single most dangerous control in the product and it sits on the first screen.
Settings finally has a home
The review found zero mentions of a settings screen anywhere in canon — not in 02, 04, 05 or the checklist. It owns the D1 tunables (02 §11.3: call-ins 12–40), appearance, Reduce Motion acknowledgement, and the save. Reachable from the title and from the last tab; nowhere else.

It passes 02 §2.2, which is the whole point
Two defensible answers: resources with a two-season clock, or time with a thin roster — and a middle option that is genuinely neither. A visible consequence: the expectation and the patience are stated as numbers before you choose, not discovered in year two. A cost: the other two jobs are gone. A first decision that fails this test teaches the player that decisions here do not matter, on the screen where they are deciding whether to keep playing.
Three, and three is the argument
The working-memory rule caps a decision at four visible options; three cards at 246 pt each is what 785 pt of width holds without a scroll. It also happens to be the number that makes the trade legible — one of each pole and one in between. A job board that scrolled would be a list, and a list is a filter problem, not a decision.
Archetype is the load-bearing word
SLEEPING GIANT, STEADY HAND, RECLAMATION come from P2's 14 generated archetypes, which IdentityDistributionTests already proves are mechanically real rather than cosmetic. This screen is the first place a player meets one, and it is the payoff for a whole phase of generation work that has so far been invisible.
Open: does the board pick three, or does the player search?
Drawn as three offered jobs. The alternative — browse all 134 programmes and apply — is a different game and a much worse first five minutes, but it is a real design question and 02 §10 does not settle it. Flagged for the owner rather than decided here.

This is where team colour earns its keep
The left third is the programme's generated primary at full bleed with its secondary as the crest and rule. It is the only full-bleed team surface outside the match, and it exists because this is the moment the player becomes this programme's coach. Every save's version of this screen is a different colour, which is exactly the differentiator P2 built and v1 never spent.
A stakeholder needs a component, and did not have one
04 §7 requires stakeholder voices "distinct enough to be recognised without a name attached", and §3's registry had nothing to say them with. StakeholderCard: initials disc, name, role, group index, and quoted speech against a rule in the speaker's colour. Four groups, four rule colours, so the speaker is identifiable before the name is read.
The bar is stated as numbers, once, here
Nine wins and two seasons appear on the board, again in the offer, and a third time from the AD's mouth. That is deliberate repetition: 02 §7's jeopardy only works if the player knew the bar before they missed it, and 01-RESEARCH.md §6.0 found the prior build's jeopardy frozen for a whole season without the player ever being told what it measured.

Icon beside label, not stacked. The stock iOS arrangement puts a label under an icon and needs ~49 pt; landscape has width to spare and height to save, so the pair goes horizontal and the bar comes down to 44 — exactly the touch floor, not a pixel more. The active tab is marked by a 2 pt live rule on its top edge, because in landscape the bar's bottom edge is against the home indicator and a bottom marker would collide with it.
The bar costs exactly one row, and here is the arithmetic
390 − 22 status − 44 bar − 21 home indicator = 303 pt of content, 78% of the screen. At 44 pt rows that is 6 rows, down from 7. At AX5, where a row is ~65 pt, it is 4 rows, down from 5. Losing a fifth of the scarce axis to navigation is the real price of a bottom bar in landscape and it should be paid knowingly. Stock iOS at 49 pt would cost the same row and 5 pt more.
The match view has no bar
The broadcast register is full-bleed, so the bar is hidden there and the field gets all 369 pt — which is also what 04 §5.2's arithmetic already assumed. Leaving the bar visible during a match would clip the field by 44 pt and break the whole-field claim.
"Tabs" is the wrong word and the docs should stop using it
You said you were not sure it is tabs, and you are right to doubt it. These are five destinations in a persistent switcher, and 04 §4 calls them tabs only because iOS does. The distinction matters once: Recruit becomes Front office after promotion, so one position in the bar changes its label, icon and contents mid-career. A tab bar implies fixed furniture; this one has a slot that mutates when the tier does. Naming it a destination bar in 04 is a one-word change that stops a builder assuming the five are static.
